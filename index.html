Years of college: 4 (a standard bachelor's degree). Cost inflation is set per sector. Recent published increases run lower (≈2.9% in-state, 3.4% out-of-state, 4.0% private for 2025-26) and public tuition has even fallen in real terms over the last decade — but over 30 years all sectors compounded well above general inflation. Public tuition is more volatile (driven by state-budget cycles); private and elite schools raise prices more steadily. The values above blend the recent sector ordering with the longer-run level for an 18-year horizon. Pick "Custom" to set your own.

Return assumptions

Mean annual return is your portfolio's expected average growth per year; volatility is how much yearly returns swing around it (one standard deviation). Both are nominal (before inflation) — the college-cost side handles rising prices. More stocks → higher expected return, but bigger swings.

Since 1928, US large-cap stocks have returned ≈10%/yr nominal with a ≈20% standard deviation; bonds ≈5%/yr with ≈5%. Forward-looking estimates from major firms tend to run lower (~6–8% for a balanced mix).

The most common 529 choice is an age-based (enrollment-date) portfolio — the default "set-it-and-forget-it" option on every plan. It isn't a fixed mix: it starts aggressive (~80–90% stocks while the child is young) and glides to conservative (~20–30% stocks near college). This tool uses one fixed assumption for the whole horizon, so pick a row for your child's age — nearer 80/20 when they're little, nearer 40/60 as college approaches.

Sources: S&P 500 historical annual returns (since 1928), S&P 500 return & standard-deviation data, and 529 investment options (age-based portfolios).
